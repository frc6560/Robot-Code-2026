# Competition autos and mechanism controls

Auto Chooser exposes all six primary choices:

| Position | Blue choice | Red choice | Source path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Left | Blue Left | Red Left | `non-human` |
| Center | Blue Center | Red Center | `hub` |
| Right | Blue Right | Red Right | `human` |

Blue Center is initially selected; select any of the six before starting auto. These are the paths imported from `autos (1).zip`. `Backup - Back Up and Shoot` selects the existing fixed 2.4 m retreat routine; `Idle` performs no autonomous action. Choose the path matching the physical starting position and alliance. Primary paths reset odometry to their first waypoint. The backup retains the measured pose.

Red paths are generated by a **180-degree rotation about field center**: `(x, y, heading)` becomes `(16.54 - x, 8.07 - y, heading + 180 degrees)`. Every waypoint and standalone translation/rotation target is transformed; segment order, events, event ratios, and path constraints remain unchanged. Both B-Line automatic flipping and mirroring are disabled for all six entries, so the selected coordinates are followed exactly and red is never transformed twice.

Competition-path profiled rotations are disabled and intermediate waypoint handoff radii are 0.30 m. The imported paths retain their exported segment speed constraints and use the existing B-Line global constraints and translation, rotation and cross-track PID constants. They use B-Line's actual `FollowPath` implementation.

Event behavior:

- `intake`: request extension and latch the intake roller request at 2500 mechanism RPM. Rollers start when extension is requested and the rack continuous-current limit is confirmed, and continue until retraction or auto cancellation.
- `rev`: run `FuelShootCommand`'s trajectory/RPM calculation and flywheel ramp, with feeding blocked. B-Line continues owning the drivetrain.
- `shoot`: stop path following and run the shared `FuelShootCommand.aimedShot` factory used by teleop right trigger, with zero requested translation. It aims at the alliance hub, then runs the same floor/tunnel RPMs and release gates. It remains active until autonomous ends; it has no timed unload cutoff. Reaching the final waypoint also starts shooting, so B-Line's endpoint tolerance cannot skip a marker at t=1.

All auto event actions are owned by the autonomous command. Cancellation ends the follower, aiming, feeding, intake and rev command; no separately scheduled event command survives the auto. The flywheel returns to idle when enabled and coasts while disabled/pit coast is active.

The default flywheel goal is 1000 RPM only inside the known alliance zone, and 0 RPM outside it or when alliance/position is unknown, in both enabled teleop and auto. Blue owns x < 4.70 m; red owns x > 11.80 m. Crossing out stops idle output. Explicit right-trigger shooting/passing still requests shooting RPM outside the zone. Right trigger requests the shooting RPM; releasing it returns to idle. Auto stays idle until `rev`/`shoot`. Manual indexer controls retain idle speed; the previous operator A/button-board 11 spin-up shortcuts are removed. The 2.4 m backup stays idle during its retreat and requests shooting RPM once stopped.

The active shooting RPM tolerance is `FuelShotConfig.RPM_TOLERANCE = 60.0` (absolute measured error must be less than 60 RPM), with `READY_DWELL_SECONDS = 0.10`. The measured trajectory scoring-band check can impose a tighter restriction. `ShooterConstants.FLYWHEEL_RPM_TOLERANCE = 200.0` is a legacy/simulation-test tolerance and does not control aimed-shot feeding.

Teleop intake controls:

| Control | Action while held |
| --- | --- |
| Left trigger | Drive outward independently of encoder position and request rollers at `IntakeConstants.ROLLER_RPM`, gated by the confirmed continuous current limit, with no encoder-extension threshold. |
| Right bumper | Force retract inward until the wall-current stop; stop rollers. |
| Left bumper | Force extend outward until the sustained-current stop; stop rollers. |
| Right trigger | Aim, spin and shoot while force retracting the intake fully. Retraction starts alongside alignment and stops at the wall. |
| A | Confirm the intake is already fully extended and stop rack output without changing its encoder reading. Does not start rollers by itself. |

Releasing either bumper or left trigger immediately stops rack and roller output and resets the boost and endpoint latches. The next press, including another attempt at the same target, gets a fresh boost. Releasing right trigger cancels shooting and its retraction, with the same reset. Releasing one control never creates a new press for a different held control. Right-bumper retraction takes priority over left-bumper forced extension, which takes priority over left-trigger collection. Intake controls can interrupt the shooting command; the command owns the intake alongside drive, shooter and indexers. Mode-transition releases cannot cancel or clear an autonomous intake request.

Left trigger no longer runs the floor/tunnel indexers. Operator button-board 3 retains manual feed. Right trigger retains aimed shooting, and A no longer aligns to the trench. Driver controls are enabled only in teleop outside pit coast. Endpoint confirmation and forced motion still require healthy rack feedback and a valid startup reference.

Every intake actuation mode, including both triggers, both forced bumper directions and autonomous requests, allows up to 60 A stator current for the first 1.5 seconds while motion is needed, then continues at 40 A until an endpoint stop or release. Repeated periodic requests do not extend the timer. Reaching the position target or stopping ends the allowance early. Forced motion has no exception to the 1.5-second boost deadline. A detected current spike stops actuator output completely and latches it off for the rest of that request; reducing the configured limit to 40 A does not restart the actuator. The rack supply-current limit remains 30 A. Rollers wait for the 40 A stator limit to be confirmed in addition to their extension gate. Failed current-limit changes stop motor output and are retried.

A wall/current spike requires stator current **strictly above 40 A for two continuous seconds**, checked at control-loop frequency with fresh feedback. A reading at or below 40 A, invalid current, stale current or lost rack readiness resets the timer. This replaces the previous two-short-spike scheme with one sustained spike:

- During normal or forced extension, the spike latches rack output off and permits requested rollers even if the encoder reports partial extension. The encoder position target and manual A confirmation remain alternative stops. After the normal boost expires the 40 A cap prevents a guaranteed above-40 A stall signal; release/retry or A confirmation remains available. Left-bumper forced extension drives outward directly, bypassing the encoder soft limit, and uses the same 1.5-second 60 A allowance, followed by 40 A until the spike or release. It does not run rollers or rewrite the encoder.
- During force retract, the spike stops the motor, sets the encoder to the physical retracted zero, and latches the motor off for the rest of that request. It does not stop merely because the old encoder zero was reached. Release before a confirmed stall does not re-zero.
- Autonomous position-retraction requests keep their encoder target; they do not use the force-retract wall detector.
- Right-trigger retraction stops and stays latched off after the spike even while the shooting/aiming command remains active; it does not repeatedly restart the retraction.
- New requests, release, disable, pit coast and auto cancellation clear endpoint latches and current timers. Feedback/configuration faults block output.

Both driver triggers now use direct actuator drive rather than encoder-position control. Left trigger drives outward and requests collection; right trigger drives inward alongside aiming/shooting. Encoder position cannot stop either trigger move or establish arrival. `FullyExtended`, `FullyRetracted` and `atDeployTarget()` require the corresponding sustained-current endpoint latch; A remains the explicitly requested manual extension-confirmation override. Encoder feedback remains available for telemetry; neither roller-control layer checks an encoder extension fraction. Autonomous position requests retain their existing control path.
